What it is

A white-box safety sidecar for self-hosted open-weight models.

If you run your own model — for cost, latency, data residency, or because you fine-tuned it — safety is your job, and the tools you're handed are text-level guard models: a second forward pass that reads the prompt or the response. probe-factory instead reads the activations your model already produced. Intent shows up there before it shows up in the output, so a linear probe catches it earlier, cheaper, and — on our data — more accurately than the guard model everyone already knows.

The approach is proven: Google DeepMind ships production probes for Gemini; Goodfire deployed probes at Rakuten at 10–500× lower cost than an LLM judge. What no one has built is the part that keeps a probe valid once it's deployed against weights that never stop changing.

For investors Why the maintenance is the moat

The failure case is the better business.

A linear probe is ~200 lines and DeepMind published the recipe — any competent ML team builds one in a week. What they cannot cheaply do is keep it valid. We measured exactly where it goes stale, and each failure a customer can't self-detect is recurring, ops-shaped revenue.

  • Fine-tuning silently breaks it — measured. A benign, general-purpose LoRA cut recall from 95% to 42% with no error and no alert; refit restores it to 0.97. The customer cannot see this happen, which is precisely why re-certification recurs.
  • Quantization does not — int8/int4 preserve the probe almost exactly, so we refuse to sell re-cert for it. Being honest about what doesn't need us is what makes the rest credible.
  • Obfuscation is a live hole with a paid fix. Leetspeak drops the base probe to chance; adversarial re-cert recovers most of it, and new encodings keep arriving.
  • The open question we're not hiding: whether re-certification reads to buyers as a valued service or a tax on a fragile approach. Customer discovery is settling that now — we won't let the moat framing paper over it.
